Smart Masjid Clock privacy policy

Effective 1 October 2026 · applies to Smart Masjid Clock for Android, version 3.7.0 and later (application ID com.namaz.smartalerts)

Published by iKhan

In short

  • The app works offline. Prayer times are calculated on your phone, for any place in the world.
  • There is no account, no advertising and no server of ours. We never sell, rent or share your data.
  • Your location, timetables, photos and settings stay on your phone, and uninstalling the app deletes them.
  • Three things can use the internet: online prayer times, which is off until you turn it on; city lists for a time zone, downloaded when you ask (or automatically, if you allow it); and Google ML Kit, the on-phone text reader for timetable photos, which sends Google diagnostics about itself.

1. What the app keeps on your phone

Everything below is stored only in the app's private storage on your phone. We do not receive it. Android's cloud backup is switched off for this app, so none of it is copied to a backup service. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes all of it.

2. Location

The app asks for location only when you use a feature that needs it, such as "Use my current location". You can instead choose your city from the city lists and never grant location. Background location ("Allow all the time") is optional: after an explanation in the app, it lets the app switch to a saved mosque's timetable, or to one of your profiles, when you arrive, even when the app is closed. Arrival is detected on the phone by Android and Google Play services. The app itself never sends your location anywhere, except the rounded position used by the optional online prayer times (section 4).

3. Profiles and Wi-Fi names

Profiles (for example Home, Office or Travel) are optional. A profile can switch on by days and times you choose, when the phone is connected to a Wi-Fi network you named, near a place you saved, or far from home. Android shows the connected Wi-Fi name only to apps with location permission. The app reads it on the phone, compares it with your profiles and keeps only the last name it read. Profiles, their places and Wi-Fi names are never sent anywhere and are deleted with the profile or the app.

4. Online prayer times (optional, off by default)

In Settings → Timetable → Automatic → Coordinates you can turn on Fetch prayer times online. The app explains it before you turn it on. When it is on, the app sends:

  • your location rounded to two decimal places (about 1 km), and
  • the calculation settings (method, Asr school and high-latitude rule)

over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to api.aladhan.com, a prayer-times service run by a third party (AlAdhan) under its own terms, to get this month's and next month's prayer start times. It asks at most about once every 20 days per month and place. Nothing else is sent: no name, account, device ID, mosque, jamaat times, photos or files. The times are saved on the phone. If the service cannot be reached, or its times differ from the phone's own calculation by more than 15 minutes, the phone's calculation is used. Turning the switch off stops all requests.

5. City lists (downloaded when you ask)

The app includes the biggest cities of every time zone. Every other city comes in one list per time zone, downloaded from the app's website only when you tap Download, or, if you turn on Download automatically when the phone is in a new time zone after an explanation in the app, when your phone is in a time zone whose list is not on the phone yet. The request asks for that time zone's file over HTTPS and sends nothing else: no location, settings, account or device ID. Like any web request, the website sees which time zone's list is asked for and your internet address. Each list is checked against a checksum built into the app before it is used. Only the most recently used lists are kept (3, 5 or 10, your choice), and in Settings → City lists & storage you can mark lists to keep or remove any of them. The website's host may keep ordinary access logs (the file asked for and the internet address) under its own terms; we do not use them, and uninstalling the app does not delete them.

6. Reading timetable photos

Text in timetable photos is read on the phone with Google ML Kit's built-in text recognizer. Photos, PDFs and the text read from them are never uploaded. Google ML Kit sends Google diagnostics and usage information about the ML Kit library itself (for example device and app version, performance and errors), which Google says can include a per-installation identifier. This does not include your photos, the text read or your location. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure.

7. Crash reports (optional, off by default)

If you turn crash reports on (Settings → About), a report is saved on the phone when the app crashes. It contains the app version, Android version, phone model, the time of the crash and the places in the app's code involved. It does not contain prayer times, places, mosque names, messages or files. A report leaves the phone only if you tap Share last report and choose where to send it. You can delete it at any time.

8. Permissions

  • Exact alarms, full-screen alerts, notifications, wake lock, starting after a restart: ring prayer alarms on time, also after the phone restarts.
  • Do Not Disturb access: silence the phone during prayers. On Android 15 and later this is the app's own Do Not Disturb mode, which never turns off a mode of yours.
  • Foreground service: keep the alarm schedule running on phones that close background apps, and play the azaan.
  • Battery settings: ask Android not to delay the alarms.
  • Location (optional) and Wi-Fi state: as described in sections 2 and 3.
  • Internet: only for the optional online prayer times, city-list downloads and Google ML Kit's diagnostics.

The app does not use the camera permission (photos come from your camera app), contacts, microphone, messages or advertising ID.

9. Sharing

Apart from ML Kit's diagnostics, the optional online prayer times and city-list downloads, the app shares nothing by itself. Something leaves the phone only when you send it yourself, for example a crash report through Android's share menu. We do not sell, rent or trade data, and there is no advertising or tracking.

10. Security

Data is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. The app has no server, so there is no copy of your data for us to lose. Online prayer times, city lists and ML Kit's diagnostics travel over encrypted connections (HTTPS), and each downloaded city list is checked before use.

11. Keeping and deleting data

Data stays on your phone until you delete it. In the app you can delete a profile, a saved mosque, a crash report, imported prayer start times or the jamaat times learned for a place; in Settings → City lists & storage you can remove downloaded city lists, saved online prayer times and saved-masjid calculations; you can turn off online prayer times and automatic downloads at any time, or delete everything by uninstalling the app or clearing its storage in Android settings. We hold no data about you on any server, so there is nothing for us to keep or delete. Data that Google ML Kit or AlAdhan receive is handled under their own policies.

12. Your choices and rights

You decide what the app may use: location, background location, Do Not Disturb access, online prayer times, city-list downloads and crash reports can each be refused or turned off without losing the rest of the app. Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the European Union and United Kingdom, or privacy laws in other countries and US states), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal data. Because the app keeps your data only on your phone, you can exercise these rights directly in the app or by uninstalling it. For questions, contact us (section 15).

13. Users around the world

The app can be used in any country. Your data is processed on your own phone. If you turn on online prayer times, the rounded location and calculation settings are sent to AlAdhan's service; city lists come from the app's website; and Google ML Kit's diagnostics are sent to Google; these services may process data in other countries under their own terms.

14. Children

Smart Masjid Clock is a general-audience prayer clock. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.
